# Moon Paracas — Antigravity Technical Redesign Brief V2

This document replaces the previous direction with a more realistic and buildable framework for the Moon Paracas masterplan. The goal is to preserve the emotional strength of the radial oasis concept while correcting the lot geometry, access logic, and density so the project can actually be developed on the official site polygon.[file:197][file:11][file:351]

## 1. Base project facts

- Official gross site area: 112,391.80 m².[file:197]
- Site condition: irregular perimeter, not a simple rectangle or ideal diamond.[file:197]
- Project model: private low-density development with progressive implementation and approximate exclusive-use areas.[file:11][file:9]
- Product intention: beautiful, modern, less-boxy, walkable, desert-linked community with roads and cars kept visually secondary to the homes and landscape.[cite:260][cite:261]

## 2. Direct diagnosis of the current radial plan

The current radial plan is attractive as an image, but it is not yet viable as a development drawing.[file:351]
The image itself shows that the system is still generating undersized and over-compressed pieces, including a visible lot in M11 with only 85.74 m², which is below even the newly accepted compact-house minimum.[file:351]
Many lots in the inner and middle fan blocks are too triangular, too narrow at one end, or too dependent on geometric leftovers rather than intentional buildable envelopes.[file:351]
This means the design currently behaves more like a filled diagram than a true residential masterplan.[file:351]

## 3. New viable design rule

A minimum lot can now be accepted at **150 m²**, but only for compact-house typologies and only when the lot shape is truly buildable.[file:351]
That minimum must not become the dominant product, because the project still needs a strong center of gravity near the 225–250 m² range to remain commercially and spatially coherent with Moon Paracas.[file:11][cite:260]
The current recalibration therefore shifts the scheme toward a more disciplined mix in which compact lots are limited, the standard product dominates, and premium edge lots absorb irregular geometry in a controlled way.[code_file:348][code_file:349][code_file:350]

## 4. Recommended lot formula

The revised viable planning model is:

- Total lots: **238**.[code_file:350]
- Estimated saleable lot area: **54,824 m²**.[code_file:350]
- Average lot area: **230.35 m²**.[code_file:350]
- Saleable area share of gross site: **48.78%**.[code_file:350]
- Remaining non-saleable area for roads, buffers, amenities, landscape, irregular residue, and real circulation margin: **57,567.80 m²**.[code_file:350]

This is less aggressive than the 55% lotization target previously explored, but it is more believable because it gives the plan enough room to solve access, geometry, and breathing space without forcing too many bad lots into the center.[code_file:350][cite:261]

## 7. Example of correct block design

Below is an example of what a **correct manzaneo** should mean in this project.

### Example Block Type A — Middle Ring Standard Block

**Use this as a reference model for blocks like M7, M8, M9, and M10.**[code_file:349]

- Block family: curved middle ring.
- Recommended count: 8 to 9 lots.[code_file:349]
- Target average size: 212–225 m².[code_file:349]
- Road condition: one clear front to an internal curved street, no hidden access pieces.
- Lot geometry: slightly trapezoidal is acceptable; needle-like, pinched, or severe wedge shapes are not acceptable.[web:332][web:333]
- Buildable logic: each lot must contain a clean house pad, outdoor area, service strip, and reasonable setback behavior.

### Reference proportions for a correct lot in this block family

| Condition | Recommended rule |
|---|---|
| Minimum area | 150 m² absolute minimum, only for compact-house cases |
| Preferred standard area | 200–225 m² |
| Preferred dominant area | 225–249 m² |
| Frontage quality | Must feel usable and readable from the street |
| Depth quality | Must not become a long unusable tail |
| Shape | Trapezoid or radial quadrilateral acceptable; repeated triangles are not |
| Access | Every lot must front a road or defined access condition |

A practical planning reference is to avoid long narrow parcels and control width-to-depth proportions so the design does not create deep unusable tails; low-density lot guidance commonly uses width-to-depth control specifically to prevent long and narrow lots.[web:332]
Planning standards also emphasize that lot size, width, depth, shape, and orientation must be appropriate to the contemplated development, which is exactly the issue in the current plan.[web:333][web:364]

## 8. What must be redrawn first

### Priority 1 — Repair over-compressed fan blocks

The first blocks to redraw are the wedge-heavy blocks where the plan visibly collapses into thin slices: M11, M12, M13, M14, M7, M8, M9, and M10.[file:351]
These blocks currently behave like a geometric fan rather than a buildable neighborhood and must be simplified with fewer, wider, cleaner lots.[file:351]

### Priority 2 — Protect the inner oasis

M1–M4 must not feel like leftover pieces wrapping a circle. They must read as special compact-villa lots facing the central amenity environment.[file:351]
These blocks should carry fewer lots, higher shape quality, and stronger premium identity despite their smaller size.[code_file:349]

### Priority 3 — Use the outer ring as relief

M15–M24 must absorb more of the geometry calmly and intentionally, especially for standard-plus and premium edge product.[code_file:349]
Irregular edge conditions should be concentrated there, because edge and reserve-ready lots can tolerate more shape variation than inner fan blocks.[code_file:349]

## 9. Access and circulation rules

The plan is not approved unless every lot has believable access.[file:197]
Antigravity must redraw the plan in this sequence:

1. Official site polygon.[file:197]
2. Internal buffer / setback band.[file:197]
3. Main entrance sequence.[file:351]
4. Primary circulation loop.
5. Secondary residential streets.
6. Block envelopes.
7. Lot lines.

No lot should depend on fake frontage, hidden residual access, or overcomplicated micro-angles to claim viability.[web:354][web:360]
Roads must solve access, but they should remain visually quiet and secondary to the houses, walking experience, and landscape.[cite:261]

## 10. Non-negotiable redraw rules

- No lot below 150 m².[file:351]
- No repeated needle lots.
- No uncontrolled triangular fragmentation.
- No block where the majority of lots taper aggressively.
- No premium marketing on lots that are only premium by color but not by geometry.
- No final approval without block-by-block frontage validation.
- No final approval without confirming that each lot supports a realistic house concept footprint.

## 12. Final instruction

Preserve the radial oasis identity, but redesign the lot puzzle with planning discipline. The next version should feel like a real Paracas desert community: calm, breathable, buildable, and commercially believable — not a beautiful diagram full of compromised pieces.[cite:260][cite:261][file:351]
